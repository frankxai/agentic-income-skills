# income-market-intel — keeping the read current

Markets decay. A catalog rate changes, a marketplace shifts its cut, a model saturates, a platform bans a workflow. This skill is how the network stays right about where the money is, instead of confidently wrong.

## The margin lens (how to rank any model)

Rank every candidate income model on five axes, in this order:

1. **Realized margin** — what clears after the true cost to run it: model tokens, hosting, and the operator's hours. Digital-only outcomes clear 70%+. The single biggest margin lever is model routing: a well-prompted smaller model handles ~70% of agent work at ~10% of the cost, which is often 10–15 margin points on its own. Encode this — route volume to Haiku/Sonnet, reserve Opus for judgment.
2. **Recurrence** — does it pay once or every month? Recurring compounds; one-time is a treadmill. This is why the catalog ranks recurring payers first.
3. **Defensibility** — what stops the next person copying it in a weekend? Judgment encoded as a repeatable system defends; a thin wrapper doesn't. The best-selling skills in-market are the ones that capture weeks of standards (a real code-review skill outsells a $3 utility), not the ones that save a keystroke.
4. **Ops/legal drag** — anything with a support SLA, refund treadmill, licensing exposure, or ToS dependency scores down hard. Autonomous income means no drag.
5. **Alignment** — fit to the specific operator's real skills and appetite for hands-on work. The highest-margin model someone can't execute is worth zero to them.

## Pricing (encode this into every offer designed)

Price on the **outcome delivered, not the cost to produce**. Where the outcome saves or earns the customer a known amount, capture a share of that value rather than a flat fee — an agent that saves a customer $10k can capture a percentage sustainably. Cost-plus pricing leaves the margin the judgment created on the table.

## The index: `awesome-agentic-income`

This is the network's accumulated, public memory of income models, tools, affiliate programs, marketplaces, and business models — ranked by margin and freshness. It is the single canonical income index; do not spawn parallel lists (`awesome-wealth-agent-skills` and `awesome-investor-agent-skills` exist but income consolidates here). The `market-researcher` agent reads it at the start of every run and writes findings back at the end, so each run starts smarter than the last.

Each entry carries: the model, the margin/number (with source or "unverified"), why it's working this quarter, the catch (legal/ops/platform risk), and a `lastVerified` date.

## The freshness loop (so a system's economics survive review)

A page or offer is not passive unless its economics still hold on review. Run on a cadence (weekly for the catalog, monthly for the market read):

1. **Re-verify the catalog** — do the joined programs still pay what they claimed? Flag rate changes, closed programs, new recurring options. (`affiliate-audit` does the link-coverage half of this.)
2. **Re-scan the market** — what rose, what saturated, what changed platform rules since last run. Update the index entries' `lastVerified`.
3. **Flag decay** — surface pages/offers whose underlying tool lost its program, dropped its rate, or got out-competed. These need a refresh or a re-route.
4. **Surface, don't auto-act** — the loop reports; the operator (or the architect with approval) acts. Never auto-publish a change, never auto-spend, never auto-send.

The loop is the difference between a system that earns for years and a directory that rots. Boredom is the point: research → verify → route → review → repeat.
